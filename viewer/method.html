DepthWizard — method and evidence

One satellite or aerial image in; a height map (DSM) and a navigable 3D model out. Every number on this page was measured on data the model never trained on, with nothing fitted to the answer.

How it works

  1. Read the image as the sensor wrote it. Band order from the file's colour tags (untagged 4-band 16-bit stacks read as Blue, Green, Red, NIR — the Cartosat MX order); 16-bit data stretched per band; nodata, transparency and black footprint borders masked everywhere downstream.
  2. Height above ground from a fine-tuned model. Depth Anything V2 Small (Apache-2.0), fine-tuned to metres on airborne LiDAR (GAMUS, DFC2019). The model always runs at the 0.3 m scale it was trained at: a 0.6 m Cartosat image is upsampled 2× first, large scenes in ~2048 px blocks.
  3. Absolute elevation from a global DEM. For a GeoTIFF: terrain from Copernicus GLO-30 (or SRTM) at 30 m, minus the measured fraction of the building/tree height the radar DEM already contains, plus the model's height. PNG/JPG without coordinates gives a relative DSM (rDSM).
  4. An error bar on every pixel. 1-sigma uncertainty from the spread of the model over rotated views and the predicted height, calibrated on held-out LiDAR.
  5. A 3D scene you can walk through. The original image draped on the terrain and buildings, roof and wall texture corrected for the image's measured lean, height/slope/uncertainty views, measurement tools, and validation against your own reference DSM.

Uncertainty and geometry

  • Uncertainty calibration on held-out views: see checkpoints/height_best/sigma_calibration.json (expected calibration error and rank correlation between sigma and actual error).
  • Projection accuracy (HITEC City, 5,822 buildings): roof outlines sit a median 0.6 m (one pixel) from the image's own edges.

India

No public LiDAR covers our Indian scenes. The absolute DSM over Hyderabad, Periyar, Shimla and rural Telangana is scored against Copernicus and SRTM (anchored to one, scored against the other) and against ICESat-2 satellite LiDAR. BENCHMARK_ISRO_PROTOCOL.md

Known limits

  • Tall forest and tall buildings are under-called: the open training data holds few of either.
  • Buildings are extruded prisms with detected rooftop structures; domes and spires are simplified.
  • 0.6 m is simulated from 0.3 m imagery; the Cartosat sensor and colour gap is not measured.
  • A relative DSM (no coordinates in the file) carries no absolute elevation.
