From one satellite image to a measured 3D city

DepthWizard reads the height of every pixel, in metres, from one nadir image, anchors it to the elevation model ISRO scores against, and builds a 3D place you can fly, walk and measure — with an uncertainty on every number.

    How to test it

    1. Open a prepared placeSeven Indian places below were reconstructed ahead of time and open in seconds: city, hills, forest, desert.
    2. Upload your own imageDrop a GeoTIFF, PNG or JPEG. The same pipeline runs live with a stage-by-stage log and timer.
    3. Read any heightPoint at a roof or slope in 3D: height above ground and its own ±, calibrated on LiDAR the model never saw.
    4. AnalyseSlope, flood level, cross-section, line of sight, “Measured only” to hide everything drawn.
    5. Take the dataDownload the DSM GeoTIFF on the input’s own grid, or upload a reference DSM to score it.

    What we do not claim

    Stated first, so nothing is discovered later

    Tall towers read lowSingle-image methods under-call towers above about 20 m; the ± grows with height and says so.
    Building heights need finer than 2 mAt 2.5–10 m a building is a few pixels; the DSM there rests on the DEM. Products say so in their metadata.
    No Indian LiDAR yetHeights are scored on held-out LiDAR abroad; in India the absolute DSM is checked against two public DEMs.
